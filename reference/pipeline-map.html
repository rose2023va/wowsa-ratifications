01

The customer buys a ratification

WooCommerce Automatic

Variable product 70664 with two variations, Solo 102119 and Relay 102120. No default is set, so the buyer must choose. The listing carries a purchase note linking to Jotform. Three add-ons are offered: Record Attempt, Live Map Tracking, Blog Recap and Social Sharing.

02

The order reaches Airtable

Airtable · Ratifications Pipeline Automatic Currently faulty

The Ratification Order automation receives a WooCommerce webhook, runs a script that matches on product ID, creates a record in Table 1, and sends the swimmer an email pointing them at the Pre-Swim Planning form.

Broken now

The script step shows Fix configuration, the payload input reads Invalid value, and the trigger test has failed twice. Two candidate causes: an unfinished attempt to add Solo and Relay handling, and a trigger that never had a sample payload captured. The second predates the first.

Two gaps at this step

Solo and relay are not separated. The script matches on product ID and never reads the variation, even though both variation IDs arrive on the line item.

Add-ons are not captured. Fields exist on Table 1 and nothing fills them, so anyone who buys Record Attempt, Live Map Tracking or Blog Recap receives no instructions for what they paid for. An extended script was drafted on 6 August and never applied, because Airtable's API refuses to edit an automation containing a script step.

03

The swimmer enters the workflow

Jotform · Pre-Swim Planning Automatic Relay never wired

The email link is a plain form URL with no parameters. The swimmer fills it in, and that submission is what enters them into the workflow. The SWIM ID is minted here, by a hidden auto-increment field feeding a calculation field, currently at index 1060.

The workflow trigger is set to start for every submission of the solo form, with no conditions. Two things fire at once: a Google Calendar entry for the planned swim date, and the Next Steps email carrying the GPS, photo, documentation and evidence standards.

Relay has never worked

A Relay Pre-Swim Planning form exists and is connected to nothing. The entry email hardcodes the solo form and the workflow has one trigger. Every relay ratification to date has run down the solo path.

Nothing joins the order to the submission

The swimmer retypes their name and email. If they use a different address from the one on their order, nothing connects the two records.

04

Route review

Jotform approval · Google Maps · email Manual Quinn decides, Rose records

What the builder shows and what happens are different things.

  • The approval request lands in the shared admin mailbox
  • Rose takes the start and finish coordinates off the submission manually
  • Rose measures the route in Google Maps and screenshots it
  • Rose emails the screenshot to Quinn, subject "Route for Approval SWIM-XXXX"
  • Quinn replies with his decision
  • Rose clicks Approve or Deny to record it

The measured distance has nowhere to live

It exists as pixels in a screenshot and a number in an email thread. It is not a value on any record.

Approved

Route Approval email to the swimmer. Continues. The email never states that route approval is not ratification.

Denied

Route Review Outcome email carrying the approver's reason, then the workflow ends. The email invites a resubmission, which mints a new SWIM ID for the same swim with nothing linking them.

05

Independent Observer Agreement

Jotform Assigned automatically Reviewed manually

The observer named on the planning form is assigned directly, with prefill on. Three pages, three signatures: Roles and Responsibilities, Rules of Engagement, Conflict of Interest. Rose reviews and decides this one herself, with no consultation.

Two defects on this form

The link labelled "WOWSA Marathon Swimming Rules & Regulations" points at the observer form itself. Observers sign an undertaking that they have read the rules, via a link that returns them to the page they are on.

Assisted versus unassisted is declared by the observer, not the swimmer. That question is hidden on the planning form, so the applicant never states it, and the category that determines record eligibility is set by the person verifying.

On approval, an Observer Confirmed email goes to Quinn, the observer and the swimmer together, since Jotform has no CC. Then a good luck email to the swimmer, which fires now rather than near the swim.

06

Swim Completed

Google Calendar · Google Drive · Jotform task Manual Invisible to the swimmer

This is the point where a person becomes the clock.

  • Notice from the calendar that a swim date has passed
  • Create the swim's Drive folder manually, named SWIM-XXXX Firstname Lastname
  • Copy in the template subfolders from the local set
  • Mark the Jotform task complete

Nothing moves until someone looks

Until this task is marked complete, the swimmer and observer cannot submit their post-swim documentation. They have finished the swim, they were told links would arrive, and nothing happens. From their side it is indistinguishable from being forgotten.

There is no queue of swims whose dates have passed, no reminder, and no list to work from. The calendar also holds the date as declared at planning, which may be months old.

Drive folder A, the swimmer and observer upload space, one per swim drive.google.com/drive/folders/[DRIVE_FOLDER_A]
07

Post-swim documentation

Jotform · Google Drive Assigned automatically Nothing is verified

The observer report is assigned first. Only once it comes back is the swimmer's narrative assigned. They run in sequence because Jotform ends the whole workflow if one of two parallel branches completes, which was tested. So a swimmer who is home and ready to write waits on an observer, for no reason connected to either task.

Only one field on the observer report is required

And it is the Swim ID. Not the GPS data. Not the observer log. Not the photographs or the start and finish videos. Not the times, coordinates, elapsed time or distances. Not the observer's attestation signature.

The Drive folder is offered as an optional file drop and Jotform cannot see into it. So the workflow can mark this step done while the evidence packet is empty, and every completeness check is Rose opening the folder and noticing.

The swimmer narrative barely links back

Swim ID is not required there, has no input mask, and prefill is off on that node. Uploads across both forms are capped near 10 MB, and observers are told to email oversized GPS files directly, which puts the most important evidence outside the system.

08

Build the ratification page

WordPress · Camtasia · Vimeo · ZeroSixZero · Drive Manual, days of work

In the Jotform builder this is one task called Complete Ratification Page, with an empty description and a single outcome. Behind it:

  • Open every photograph, read its metadata timestamp, rename the file to that timestamp
  • Assemble the video in Camtasia with the standing title template, upload to Vimeo, configure the player to hide everything but play and volume
  • Upload the GPX to ZeroSixZero as a Route, create a map, add it to Live Tracks and Map Layers
  • Transcribe the observer log entry by entry in the code editor, around seventy per swim
  • Write and assemble ten page sections, choosing the photographs for each
  • Link every submitted document from Drive manually

The evidence standard depends on metadata the collection method destroys

Photo timestamps are the source of truth. Photos routinely arrive stripped, because they were passed between devices or re-saved. Both workflow emails warn about this in detail and it happens anyway. Real time capture forms were tried and did not hold.

09

Committee review

Jotform · WordPress · email Manual

The committee form is cloned manually for every swim, because Jotform caps submissions per form and a review needs to close at three. Rose edits the Swim ID and Swimmer Name defaults on each copy, embeds it in the page's Committee Review section, publishes the post private with a password, and emails that link out.

The committee reviews the page, not a summary

A reviewer opens one page, reads the full ratification, and submits their determination from the bottom of it. This already works the way a reviewing body should.

Twelve people are asked, three reviews are needed

Nobody is assigned. Whichever three respond first decide that swim. The password is one static string, [COMMITTEE_PASSWORD], sent to all twelve alongside a list of which swims are pending.

Each reviewer gives a four-point assessment on photo and video evidence, observer logs, GPS tracks, and an overall recommendation, with notes on each. Three reviewers produce twelve graded assessments.

Once all three are in, Rose builds two League Table entries manually, types in the committee names, uploads their signatures, deletes the embedded form and inserts the tables.

The grading does not survive publication

The published table shows a tick. "Approve with qualifications" and "Approve, no issues" look identical, and every note a reviewer wrote lives only inside the Jotform submission.

10

Ratified or Not Ratified

Jotform approval Manual Records the committee's outcome

The approval email says it plainly: "Please update committee decision below." This node is where an outcome reached elsewhere gets transcribed so the workflow can branch.

This is where twelve assessments become one word

Rose reads all of it and converts it into Ratified or Not Ratified using judgment. There is no rule, no threshold, no tie-break, and no record of the reasoning beyond an optional comment. Nobody has ever written down what combination of assessments ratifies a swim.

Require Login is off, so this can be recorded by clicking a button in an email.

Ratified

Certificate and socials task, then the confirmation email, then the workflow ends.

Not Ratified

One email, then the workflow ends. It offers a 30 day appeal with no path back in, and points the swimmer to the page for "the committee's full notes", which shows ticks.

11

Certificate, cards and the social post

Canva · Google Drive · Jotform Manual Diverged from the workflow

Assets are made manually in Canva. A new subfolder called "certificate and social cards" goes inside the swim's Drive folder, the link is pasted into the Jotform task, and the confirmation email goes out. The final social post is assembled from a screenshot of the finished ratification page, with the details retyped underneath. Everything then goes to Quinn, who handles the posting.

The built workflow no longer matches the agreed process

Quinn agreed that Not Ratified swims should still receive a certificate and social assets, published as a "verified attempt". That step could not be added to the workflow, so it is done manually over email. Anyone reading the builder today would conclude a Not Ratified swimmer gets an email and nothing else.

Three problems in the confirmation email

The distance quoted is the GPS track distance, not the straight-line tangent, in a message headed RATIFICATION SUMMARY. WOWSA's standard is the tangent, and that figure is collected on the observer report but not used.

It says "I've copied the ratification committee" and the recipient list is the swimmer alone. It also labels the identifier RAT # and fills it with the SWIM ID, which is worth knowing before anyone proposes minting a separate ratification number.

The thread that runs underneath

Throughout every step above, the Workflow table in Airtable is updated entirely by hand. The order automation writes to a different table. Jotform does not write to it. Drive does not write to it. Every status is typed in by a person after doing the work somewhere else, which makes it a parallel account of the process rather than the process itself. It is also what a swimmer's status gets checked against when they ask where their ratification stands.

Three things the tooling cannot do

These are hard limits, not things that were never set up. Each one is already the cause of manual work described above.

Platform limit

No scheduling against a date

Every email fires on an event, never on a date. So the good luck message arrives when the observer agreement clears rather than before the swim, there is no reminder on the 30 day deadline, no nudge when a case stalls, and no way to detect a broken case, because detecting one means noticing that time has passed.

Platform limit

No parallel steps

Tested, not assumed. Building the post-swim observer report and swimmer narrative as parallel branches ended the workflow as soon as one completed. The long single chain is not a design decision, it is the only shape available.

Platform limit

No CC or BCC

Copying anyone means listing them as a direct recipient. On the Observer Confirmed email that puts Quinn, the observer and the swimmer in one To line, visible to each other.

Where it fails without telling anyone

In each of these the system reports success and a person has to know better.

Highest risk

The assigned link is the only thread

Everyone must use the form link in the email they were sent. Use the public URL, a bookmark or a forwarded link and the submission lands outside the workflow instance. The case then waits forever for something that will never arrive, and a broken case looks exactly like a slow one.

Highest risk

Evidence can be entirely absent

The observer report requires nothing but a Swim ID, and Jotform cannot see into the Drive folder. A swim can reach committee review with an empty evidence packet and nothing in the system will say so.

Structural

Denials and appeals have no way back

A route denial ends the workflow and a corrected plan returns as a new submission with a new SWIM ID. A Not Ratified decision ends the workflow and offers a 30 day appeal that has nowhere to go. Both invite a next step and close the door behind themselves.

Structural

No amendment trail

The confirmation email says "if you'd like any tweaks to the page or assets, just reply here." Corrections after publication arrive as replies and are handled manually, with no record of what changed or when.
